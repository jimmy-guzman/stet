## Switch themes

Press `t` to open the theme switcher. Type to filter by name. Move to a theme, or hover over it, to preview it on the whole UI. Press `enter` to apply the theme, or press `esc` to go back to the previous theme.

By default, stet uses `system`, a theme that it builds from the colors of your terminal. This theme follows the light or dark appearance of your terminal. The switcher also lists these themes:

- `stet-dark` and `stet-light`, the themes of stet
- The `opencode-*` and `cmux-*` pairs
- The editor themes bundled with highlights, such as Catppuccin, Dracula, GitHub, Gruvbox, Nord, and Tokyo Night
- `mono-dark` and `mono-light`, all grey. stet starts on this pair when `NO_COLOR` has a non-empty value and `FORCE_COLOR` is not set. `FORCE_COLOR=0` or `FORCE_COLOR=false` also selects this pair.

![The theme switcher listing themes with color swatches, previewing the highlighted one live](/screenshots/theme-switcher.png)

A theme change lasts for the session only. To keep it, press `ctrl-s`. stet writes the highlighted theme and any other changed settings to your configuration. Each write is a small edit that keeps your comments.

To add your own theme, put a Zed theme file in `~/.config/stet/themes/`. If `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME` is set, use `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/stet/themes/`. stet makes all its UI colors from the palette of the theme. The [configuration reference](/docs/reference/configuration#themes) explains how.
